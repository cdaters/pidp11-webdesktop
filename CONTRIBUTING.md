# Join the workbench

Thanks for taking a look. You do not need a physical PiDP-11 or deep Linux knowledge to help.

Useful contributions include terminal-compatibility reports, historical peripheral references, clearer instructions, authentic screenshots, additional guest experiments, hardware experience and small code improvements. Questions and unfinished ideas are welcome in Discussions; reproducible bugs and proposed changes fit Issues.

For a bug report, tell us the browser/OS, project commit or checkpoint, what you tried and what happened. Say whether it involved the local simulation, an isolated guest lab or your own hardware. Include a small example or screenshot when useful. Remove tokens, passwords, private addresses and personal guest data first. Please use [private reporting](SECURITY.md) for security issues.

For code or documentation, fork the repository, make a focused change and open a pull request. Explain the problem, the resulting behavior and the checks you actually ran. There is no elaborate proposal process for small improvements. For a larger feature, start a discussion so we can share context and avoid duplicating work.

The [developer guide](docs/technical/development.md) covers local setup and tests. Keep the compact Go/TypeScript/HTML5/SVG architecture, bounded resources and truthful simulation labels. Preserve original notices and historical checkpoint tags. Never make the ordinary console, physical controls or guest lifecycle depend on a browser window. Changes involving a real installation should be tested only on hardware/media you own and have deliberately prepared for that work; our lab results are not production permission.

Original contributions are accepted under the project's [MIT License](LICENSE). Keep attribution and separate terms for third-party material. For photographs or other reference material, include the source and what permission or terms accompany it. One asset question should not prevent unrelated improvements.

Patient explanations, curiosity and respect for the people who built and preserve these machines matter more than polished jargon. We look forward to what you discover.
