# M5.22 — Practical equipment operation, history and recovery

Completed on 2026-11-23 in disposable Mac-hosted ARM64 Linux.
Implementation checkpoint: `b265d20`; documentation follows separately on main. This milestone turns
the M5.21 LP11/PC11 subset into a repeatable local operator workflow with bounded
job history and explicit data recovery. It is development main, not a release,
owner acceptance or physical installation. No physical Pi, SSH, GPIO, website,
upstream repository or AI service was accessed. Source Alpha 1/2 remain unchanged.

## What a user can now do

The [local operator guide](../technical/equipment-operator.md) supplies one procedure
from an independently prepared guest and empty spool to verified completed output.
`equipment-supervisor -prepare` records an existing verified process without starting
it. `-guided` displays its operator-attested guest/slots and accepts a small local
vocabulary: begin, returned, reader, drained, closed, complete, detach and quit.
It reuses the existing strict ownership, generation, completion and expiry checks.
It has no SIMH monitor interface or media-attachment command.

The existing printer and tape windows add **Job history**, alongside simulation,
observation and completed exports. Up to eight terminal jobs retain distinct device,
job/session identity, guest attestation, host timestamps, state, lengths, provenance
and checksums. Users explicitly select a job; technical identifiers are under Job
details. Raw bytes and printer text remain exact. Completed, expired, interrupted
and incomplete records are distinguishable; viewing history grants no live authority.
Top/Latest navigation respects real form feeds, and the job selector fits narrow windows.

The separate `equipment-records` utility saves immutable records in an explicitly
configured owner-only directory. Each file has a metadata checksum and a retained-byte
checksum, is independently reread after saving, and cannot be overwritten. The
archive refuses a ninth record; it never silently deletes or rotates data. Explicit
recovery adds retained data only. No credentials, owner nonce, PID renewal, active
session or guest-control authority is restored. No database or background persistence
service was added. [Architecture and limits](../technical/equipment-history.md).

### Partial retention and actual service restart

A third, explicitly approved job used a fresh empty punch at the same path. WebKit
observed actual output, then the independent guided supervisor received `quit`.
That process exited zero without signaling SIMH. Approval expired **4,820 ms** later.
The browser retained **40,960 bytes** as incomplete and kept completed download
disabled. A fresh supervisor process attempted completion without ownership and
exited with “no active owned session.” [Expiry](../../lab/m522/results/expiry.json),
[restart refusal](../../lab/m522/results/supervisor-restart.json).

The expired record was explicitly saved alongside the four completed records. The
actual observer process was then stopped by its verified PID/executable; its Unix
socket disappeared. The original RSX console still answered `DEV PR:`. A new
observer started with **empty history, detached equipment and detached terminal
lines**. Nothing was automatically adopted, recovered or reattached.
[Startup state](../../lab/m522/results/observer-restart.json).

Five explicit recover commands validated saved metadata and bytes. All three browsers
then downloaded all five records exactly: **15 downloads**, including three incomplete
prefixes. Recovered records retained their original identities, states and timestamps.
Old live URLs still returned 404. The later independent close of the third punch
flushed its full 44,341 bytes, but the expired record remained incomplete and was
not retroactively promoted. [Recovery evidence](../../lab/m522/results/history-after-recovery.json).

This qualifies controlled observer restart and saved-data recovery, not durability
through host power failure or automatic recovery after an arbitrary service crash.
M5.21's real supervisor-kill evidence is preserved separately. Unsaved RAM history
and active tails can be lost on observer exit; the independent host spool remains
operator-owned, with no automatic completion inferred from its presence.
