## A busier desk while the hardware waits

The waiting time has given me room to explore what those windows can actually do. UNIX V7 now draws a real Tektronix plot in the browser, and I can open vi in 2.11BSD and edit a file. Seeing a full-screen editor behave properly is more satisfying than another convincing-looking login prompt.

The printer and paper-tape experiments have moved forward too. Small PDP-11 programs drive the original emulated LP11 and PC11 devices while the browser follows the resulting bytes. That gives me genuine device output to work with, although getting a guest operating system’s printer driver into the picture is still a separate job. The ordinary disk and tape windows remain demonstrations.

I’ve also been building the sort of reference desk I wish I had beside an unfamiliar machine: searchable historical notes, source passages I can inspect, and an exact octal calculator. A local AI tutor is optional. It can be useful, but testing has also caught confident mistakes. The source facts and calculator work independently of it, and the small claim checker cannot verify a whole answer.

Rolf Peters’ independently developed Hacker Replicant helped inspire the companion’s direction. His application has now held actual laboratory conversations through my model gateway using an explicitly modified, read-only version. That is an interesting experiment, not a merged project or an announcement of supported integration. His Screen-based operator-console approach and my separate guest terminal lines still have different jobs.

For this repository refresh, I captured a new equipment desk, real V7 sessions and a fresh BSD editor session. I’ve kept the older pictures and reports as well. I want someone arriving at the project for the first time to see what they can try today, what is still a simulation, and what needs more work.
